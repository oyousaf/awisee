A generalist SEO agency arriving in iGaming for the first time tends to
make the same handful of mistakes, usually because the standard
playbook assumes constraints that simply don't apply here.

## Content that reads as generic gets penalised, not just ignored

In most verticals, thin or generic content underperforms. In iGaming,
regulators and search engines alike treat certain categories of vague
or misleading claims about odds, bonuses or outcomes as a red flag,
not just a quality signal. Content needs to be accurate as well as
optimised.

## Link sources need a second layer of vetting

A backlink from an irrelevant or low-quality site is a wasted
opportunity in most industries. In iGaming, a link from the wrong kind
of site — one operating without a licence in the relevant jurisdiction,
for instance — can create an association you don't want, independent of
any SEO value.

## Jurisdiction changes the entire strategy

Search intent, competitive landscape and even permissible advertising
language shift meaningfully between jurisdictions in ways that a
single global SEO strategy doesn't account for. A programme built for
one regulated market rarely transfers cleanly to the next without
rework.

## What this means in practice

Specialisation isn't a nice-to-have positioning line for an agency
working in this space — it's the difference between a strategy that
survives a compliance review and one that doesn't.

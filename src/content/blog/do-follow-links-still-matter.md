Every few years, a wave of commentary declares backlinks dead as a
ranking factor. They aren't — what's dead is the low-effort version of
link building: directory submissions, private blog networks, and links
bought in bulk from sites that exist solely to sell them.

## What changed, and what didn't

Search engines have become considerably better at identifying link
schemes and discounting them. What hasn't changed is that a genuine
editorial link — one placed because an editor judged it useful to their
readers — remains difficult to fake at scale, which is exactly why it
still carries weight.

## The practical distinction

The question worth asking about any prospective link isn't "will this
pass as legitimate?" but "would this placement exist if search engines
didn't exist at all?" A link earned through a genuinely useful guest
contribution, a data study a journalist wanted to cite, or a mention in
an industry roundup passes that test. A link bought from a site whose
only readership is other link buyers does not.

## Where this leaves link building strategy

Fewer, better-targeted placements consistently outperform a high-volume
campaign built on marginal sites — both for search performance and for
the much simpler reason that a marginal placement rarely sends any
actual referral traffic either.
